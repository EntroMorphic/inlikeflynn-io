The Problem

Equipment talks in telemetry.

From sub-sea to deep space, equipment talks in telemetry: vibration, current, temperature, pressure, flow, timing. The signals are everywhere. The on-device detector that listens to the equipment has been missing. Until now.

01

High decision latency

The signal travels to a distant server and the verdict travels back. For a pump that can wreck itself in seconds, the round trip is the failure.

02

No labeled training data

Supervised models demand labeled failures. Industrial equipment runs for years between them, so the labels never accumulate. The training set does not exist.

03

Equipment lacks connectivity

The most consequential machines run where backhaul is intermittent, costly, or gone. A cloud-bound detector goes dark exactly when it is needed most.

04

Monitoring tools exceed firmware constraints

Monitoring stacks are built for dashboards: large, dynamic, hungry for external services. A microcontroller offers kilobytes and a fixed budget.

How Flynn works

Learn & detect. Fire & forget.

Install. Deploy. Walk away. After the equipment normal operation envelope has been learned, and every alert marks a real, sustained departure from the conditions present at installation.

Phase 01 · Learning

Observe nominal. Lock-in thresholds.

Post enrollment, Flynn watches in silence. It builds a statistical model of normal behavior — variance, autocorrelation, distribution shape, dynamics — and commits to one conservative threshold.

Phase 02 · Detection

Zero-latency, real-time sampling.

Thereafter every sample is scored against that model. The threshold never moves, so a slow-developing fault cannot dissolve quietly into a new normal.
