FLYNN.

Deterministic anomaly detection embedded at the edge of the world — and beyond.

Flynn is a patent-pending embedded anomaly detector that lives on the microcontroller already inside the equipment it monitors — self-calibrating, fully offline, and deterministic to the bit. It is software: a compact binary, compiled for the hardware already inside your equipment — chips that cost under ten dollars.

01 The problem

Critical equipment rarely warns you before it fails.

From a subsea wellhead to a satellite reaction wheel, from autonomous orbital guidance and control systems to medical implants — a fault can turn catastrophic in milliseconds: often too distant for intervention, too fast for a human to react, and far too brief for a round-trip to the cloud. Local, deterministic, and auditable intelligence is essential.

04 How it works

Four stages for continuous, autonomous detection.

STAGE 01
Deploy
Drop a compiled binary onto the chip already inside the equipment.
STAGE 02
Enroll
Learn the healthy operating envelope — ~1,700 samples, about two seconds.
STAGE 03
Lock
Freeze the baseline permanently. A slow fault can't teach Flynn to accept it.
STAGE 04
Observe
Check every subsequent reading against the frozen reference. Forever.
